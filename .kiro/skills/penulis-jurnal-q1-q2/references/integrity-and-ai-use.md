## 1. Source and claim ledger

For substantive claims, maintain a ledger with:

| Claim | Evidence or source | Exact support checked | Intended section | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [claim] | [table, file, DOI, URL] | yes/no | [section] | verified/pending |

Use primary papers and official documentation for technical claims. Verify title, author list, venue, year, volume, pages, and DOI before adding a reference. A search snippet or AI-generated bibliography is not verification.

Check that the cited source supports the exact statement. Do not cite a review for a numerical result when the original experiment is available. Do not describe a paper's hyperparameters, seed policy, dataset split, or conclusions unless those details were inspected.

Use direct quotations sparingly. Technical manuscripts should normally synthesize evidence in original wording with attribution.

## 2. Originality and text recycling

IJCCE submissions are screened for overlapping text, but the journal publishes no universal acceptable percentage. Do not optimize prose for a numeric similarity threshold. Inspect overlap source by source:

- attributed quotations and standard nomenclature;
- necessary methodological wording;
- unattributed copying or close paraphrase;
- reuse from the author's thesis, preprint, conference paper, or prior article;
- reused data or figures without disclosure.

When converting a thesis into an article:

- design a new article argument and contribution boundary;
- write from evidence notes rather than copying thesis paragraphs;
- disclose the thesis relationship where required;
- cite related prior outputs when appropriate;
- verify copyright and institutional repository conditions;
- explain what the article adds or reorganizes.

Do not use synonym substitution to disguise copied sentence structure. If a passage cannot be expressed independently without losing its meaning, quote and cite it or obtain permission as applicable.

## 3. Responsible AI use

Classify AI assistance before finalizing the manuscript:

- `none`: no AI assistance;
- `grammar-only`: spelling, punctuation, or basic grammar checking without substantive restructuring;
- `substantive`: organization, drafting, translation, literature synthesis, or material restructuring;
- `research-method`: AI used in study design, code development, data analysis, or a reproducible research workflow.

Under the Elsevier journal policy verified in June 2026:

- substantive manuscript assistance requires an AI declaration;
- basic spelling/grammar checking does not require declaration;
- research-method use belongs in Methods with tool, version, developer, purpose, inputs, oversight, and validation where relevant;
- AI cannot be an author or cited as an author;
- authors remain responsible for accuracy, sources, originality, bias, privacy, and rights.

Keep an AI-use log containing tool/service, model or version when known, date, purpose, affected sections, source material supplied, human checks, and final author decisions.

For substantive use, prepare a declaration immediately before the references that identifies the tool, explains its limited purpose, states that the authors reviewed and edited the output, and accepts responsibility for the final article. Adapt the wording to actual use; never claim `grammar-only` when organization or content changed.

Do not target an AI-detector score. Detectors are not reliable evidence of authorship, and deliberate evasion conflicts with transparent research practice. Improve authenticity through verifiable reasoning, author review, and original expression.

## 4. Images and visual evidence

Apply the current Elsevier categories:

- Explanatory diagrams may receive AI assistance if authors verify them and disclose the tool and use in the caption and general declaration.
- Data visualizations must be derived directly from underlying data through a reproducible process. Never invent or alter data to improve a figure.
- Primary research images must not be generated or substantively altered with generative AI.
- General-purpose generative AI must not be used for graphical abstracts; use licensed scientific/professional illustration tools permitted by current policy.

Retain raw data, plotting code, original images, and transformation history. Ensure licenses permit publication.

## 5. Authorship, conflicts, and permissions

- List only humans who meet authorship criteria and approve the submission.
- Assign CRediT roles according to actual contributions.
- Obtain all author approvals before submission or preprint release.
- Disclose financial and personal competing interests using the required declaration workflow.
- State funding and whether the funder influenced design, analysis, writing, or submission.
- Obtain permission for copyrighted figures, tables, instruments, or long excerpts.
- Respect dataset licenses, consent, privacy, access restrictions, and biometric-data sensitivity.

Do not upload confidential third-party manuscripts, restricted datasets, personally identifiable information, or copyrighted content to an external AI service without authorization and terms that protect confidentiality and rights.

## 6. Final integrity gate

Block final submission if any of the following remains:

- fabricated or unverified reference/data;
- unresolved authorship or conflict disclosure;
- test leakage or undisclosed protocol change;
- copied or closely paraphrased text without attribution;
- AI use described inaccurately or omitted when required;
- figure or dataset rights not established;
- research-image manipulation inconsistent with policy;
- claim stronger than the recorded evidence.
